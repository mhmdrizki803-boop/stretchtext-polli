Stretched

Stretchtext, first described by Ted Nelson in 1967, is a way of reading and writing where a single sentence can open up into a longer explanation directly beneath itself. Instead of jumping to another page, the reader simply clicks and the text grows in place, adding examples, reasoning, and context exactly where it is most useful. When the extra detail has served its purpose, it collapses back into the original compact passage. Early pioneers found the idea compelling but impractical: authors would have needed to hand-write every expansion level for every passage, and the effort was simply not sustainable. Years later, text-generating models removed that bottleneck entirely. A model can expand any dense sentence on demand, which means the oldest problem of stretchtext — the cost of writing the extra levels — disappears, and the 1967 idea finally becomes practical.

How it works

  1. Select 3–600 characters on any page.
  2. Click the floating Stretch button.
  3. The passage expands to ~3× length via Pollinations text models.
  4. You pay with your own Pollen — no extension servers.
